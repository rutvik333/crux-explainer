# Crux Explainer

Build original short-form explainers for Crux Uncovered. Read [research and publishing](references/research-and-publishing.md) for topic choice, evidence, ongoing technology checks, and YouTube policy. Read [production](references/production.md) for storyboards, render, audio, captions, and final review. Follow a narrower request such as metadata or a voice swap at its stated scope.

1. When choosing a topic, date the YouTube research and compare a small shortlist on audience interest, evidence quality, a visible mechanism, and room for an original angle. For a supplied topic, retain it and use the research to sharpen the angle. If research access is limited, record what you verified and qualify conclusions.
2. Write a claim ledger before locking the script. Verify the hook, causal explanation, quantities, and visuals against primary or authoritative sources. Rewrite or remove unsupported claims. Label approximations, simulations, and exaggerated scale when a viewer could otherwise take them literally.
3. Script a visible question, a demonstration, and a payoff. Plan the key pose, camera move, and sound event per beat. Let the animation carry the explanation. Use captions, essential identifiers, and scale notes as needed. Keep the screen clean and reserve restrained glow for a meaningful light source or reveal.
4. Generate narration with an authorized, commercially usable voice. Brian on Eleven v4 with Creative delivery and automatically inserted scene-specific audio tags is the preferred starting point where available. Use returned timings and verify the actual read. Mix soothing music and distinct scene-synced effects under intelligible speech. Finish with a roughly 2.5-second visual subscribe card, without spoken CTA.
5. Review the rendered video on a phone-sized view from start to finish. Recheck captions against speech, visual claims against evidence, and audio cues against actions. Fully decode the master and check dimensions, framing, loudness, and CTA length. Run [the FFmpeg verification helper](scripts/verify_short.py) when available.
6. Prepare the master MP4, a vertical thumbnail, caption file or caption timing, and copy-ready title, description, relevant hashtags, tags, and pinned comment when producing a complete Short. Include the source and rights records and any unresolved checks. Publish only when requested and authorized.

Use the existing editable scene and approved assets for targeted revisions. For toolkit and asset choices, run the dated freshness check in the research reference during each production day. Keep established tools where a small comparison shows they still give the better result. A daily unattended review requires a separately configured schedule.
