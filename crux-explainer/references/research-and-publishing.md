# Research and publishing

## Find the angle on YouTube

- When the user delegates topic selection, start with three to five questions in the channel's science and engineering lanes. Use public YouTube search and [YouTube Studio Trends](https://support.google.com/youtubecreatorstudio/answer/11962757) if account access is available. Record queries, date, examples, and what their evidence actually shows. Views, recency, and upload age are context. Avoid inferring audience retention or search volume from public views.
- Look for an unanswered question or a clearer demonstration. Review publicly available hooks, cuts, thumbnails, and titles to learn what viewers are promised and how quickly they see it. Use original wording, assets, staging, narration, and explanation. Report which material was available if playback or transcripts could not be inspected.
- Favor topics with a quick visual proof. For example, a train wheelset shifting sideways makes rolling-radius differences visible. A transparent tank shows what fraction of an ice cube sits underwater. Select the strongest angle you can verify and render well.

## Claim ledger and rights record

Keep one row per consequential claim. Record script line or shot, precise claim, URL and source title, evidence, date checked, and what changes in the simplified visual. Prefer original research, official agencies, standards organizations, museums, or universities. Corroborate contested claims or material numbers when possible. Use qualifiers and conditions as the source uses them. Review the final narration, graphic labels, visual mechanism, thumbnail promise, and metadata against the ledger.

Record provenance for footage, models, fonts, textures, narration voice, music, and effects. Record the applicable license or account plan at generation, commercial use and attribution terms, and a copy or link to permission. Treat unknown rights as unresolved and replace the asset if feasible. A license and YouTube's reused-content review answer separate questions.

## Daily production freshness check

On each production date, check relevant official changelogs or release notes for the current renderer, compositor, voice model, plugins, and key asset sources. Include equipment only when the project uses it. Record the date, current baseline, candidate version or asset, source URL, and decision. Check YouTube policy and upload changes before preparing a publish-ready package. If current sources are unavailable, record the last verified date and the gap.

Trial a promising change on a representative shot or voice passage. Compare fidelity, frame stability, caption timing, render time, cost, rights, and compatibility. Keep a reproducible baseline and only adopt a change that improves the intended outcome. Recheck the final export after switching.

## YouTube pre-upload review

Check the official [channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392), [Shorts revenue rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220), [advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278), and [synthetic-content disclosure guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491) at the time of upload. Apply disclosure and audience settings according to the actual video. Keep source projects, scripts, research, and rights evidence to document authorship. Review the channel's wider output for repetitious or mass-produced episodes. Each Short should have its own question, original creative contribution, and topic-specific visual proof.

Use an accurate search phrase in a short title and the opening description. Add a few relevant hashtags and upload-field tags. Choose a single readable thumbnail subject from the demonstrated mechanism or reveal. If Studio offers custom Shorts thumbnails on the account, use the vertical JPG. Otherwise suggest the strongest frame selectable through the current YouTube app. Review any copyright and ad-suitability feedback shown in Studio when access and authorization are available. Log checks as passed, fixed, pending, or unavailable with evidence. YouTube decides channel eligibility and individual ad suitability.
